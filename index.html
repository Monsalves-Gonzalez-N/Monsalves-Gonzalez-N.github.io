Nicolás Monsalves

Astronomer · University of Tor Vergata

I am Nicolás Monsalves, an astronomer with a PhD from the University of La Serena. I am currently a research fellow at the University of Tor Vergata, Italy, within the ERC-funded BHianca Group. I am passionate about data-driven methods and how to extract answers from the vast amounts of data available today, applying state-of-the-art algorithms to open problems in astronomy. During my PhD I focused on massive stars and stellar variability, and during my postdoc I am centering my work on chasing transients and observational techniques.

My Research

Ongoing work

Finding kilonovae in the Nancy Grace Roman Space Telescope with transformers In progress

I am developing a transformer-based methodology to identify kilonova candidates in the Roman survey. With a 5-day cadence, a kilonova is caught in only a handful of epochs, so the model has to classify from very few multi-band points. Attention over those epochs shows which detections and upper limits drive the decision.

A Claude Code skill for fast VLT ToO trigger decisions In use

Our group runs a Large Programme at the VLT, and when a GCN circular arrives someone has to decide within minutes whether the target is worth triggering. I built a Claude Code skill that takes the raw circular and returns a single quicklook report: the localization region and the galaxies inside it with their chance-coincidence probabilities, nested finding charts, magnitude decay predictions, and the visibility from Paranal. It turns a scattered, manual half-hour into one command. The skill is finished and now part of our observing shifts, where I keep it maintained.

Characterizing the variability of massive stars with TESS In progress

Here I apply the convolutional neural network I developed during my PhD to characterize the variability of 3557 OB stars observed by TESS across 71 sectors. The model classifies a phase-folded light curve in milliseconds, so we do not have to commit to a single best period per star. We fold and inspect every candidate period from Lomb-Scargle and autocorrelation, almost half a million in total, and the classifier tells us which ones are periodic variations.

Publications

Application of Convolutional Neural Networks to time domain astrophysics. 2D image analysis of OGLE light curves.

In this work we develop an image recognition model using convolutional neural networks with compact 2D image representations of light curves, enabling the classification of half a million sources in 180 seconds with state-of-the-art accuracy.

Astrophotometric Search for Massive Stars in the Milky Way: Confronting Random Forest Predictions with Available Spectroscopy

In this work we present a catalog of moderate to high probability massive star candidates in the Milky Way. We extended the Hot Luminous OBA star candidate catalog using Gaia DR3 data and developed a balanced random forest algorithm to identify O and early B-type stars.

About Me
